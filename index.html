Practical 2 (Ferns)

Note: The taxonomic classification presented in this practical is arranged in the following order: Class → Order → Family → Genus.

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Polypodiaceae, Aglaomorpha sp.

Key Features:
- Large epiphytic fern (grows on trees or rocks).
- Has two types of fronds: sterile nest-like fronds forming a basket, and fertile long arching fronds.
- Rhizomes often thick, creeping, covered with brown scales.

Habitat:Tropical forests, often on trunks or rocks

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Blechnaceae, Blechnum

Key Features:
- Terrestrial ferns with erect or creeping rhizomes.
- Fronds usually leathery, with clear separation between sterile and fertile fronds.
- Fertile pinnae are narrower and more elongated.

Habitat:Moist forest floors, stream banks

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Davalliaceae, Davallia

Key Features:
- Known as “rabbit’s foot fern” due to hairy creeping rhizomes.
- Rhizomes often exposed, covered with brown or silvery hairs.
- Fronds finely divided and delicate in appearance.

Habitat:Epiphytic or lithophytic (rocks).

Taxonomy:Lycopodiopsida, Lycopodiales, Lycopodiaceae, Lycopodiella

Key Features:
- Creeping stems with upright fertile branches.
- Has distinct cone-like strobili at tips.
- Leaves small, scale-like or needle-like.

Habitat:Tropical forests, often on trunks or rocks

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Nephrolepidaceae, Nephrolepis

Key Features:
-Long arching fronds, often 1–2 meters long.
- Pinnae narrow and opposite; sori round and located near vein ends.
- Creeping stolons allowing rapid spreading.

Habitat:Moist shaded areas, forest edges, also common ornamental.

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Blechnaceae, Parablechnum, Parablechnum minus

Key Features:
- Medium-sized terrestrial fern.
- Sterile and fertile fronds look different (fertile fronds taller and narrower).
- Fronds simple-pinnate with firm texture.

Habitat:Wet, shaded areas along streams and forest floors.

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Polypodiaceae, Phymatosorus

Key Features:
- Fronds often leathery with scattered round sori.
- Rhizomes creeping and covered with scales.
- Fronds may be deeply lobed depending on species.

Habitat:Lowland tropical forests, often epiphytic or growing on rocks.

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Polypodiaceae, Platycerium

Key Features:
- Known as “staghorn ferns.”
- Two types of fronds:
- Basal fronds (shield-like, clasp the tree).
- Fertile fronds (forked, antler-shaped).
- Forms large clumps on tree trunks.

Habitat:Tropical rainforests.

Taxonomy:Lycopodiopsida, Selaginellales, Selaginellaceae, Selaginella

Key Features:
- Creeping or upright branching stems forming mats.
- Four-ranked leaves: two dorsal, two ventral rows.
- Strobili present in some species, producing spores of two types (microspores & megaspores).

Habitat:Moist shaded forest floors, mossy rocks, sometimes dry habitats depending on species.

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Pteridaceae, Adiantum

Key Features:
- Fan-shaped or wedge-shaped leaflets, thin and bright green
- Black, wiry leaf stalks and rachis
- Delicate, airy, pinnate fronds
- Slightly lobed or wavy leaflet edges

Habitat:Shaded, moist, and humid environments.Widely grown as an indoor ornamental fern in low-light areas

Taxonomy:Lycopodiopsida, Lycopodiales, Lycopodiaceae, Huperzia

Key Features:
- Upright, tufted stems with spirally arranged, needle-like leaves.
- No strobili (unlike most lycophytes); spores produced in leaf axils.
- Often appears “miniature conifer-like.”

Habitat:Montane forests, mossy environments.

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Aspleniaceae, Asplenium

Key Features:
- Long, simple, strap-like fronds with entire margins
- Prominent dark midrib running along the frond
- Fronds emerge in a rosette form, resembling a “bird’s nest”
- Thick, leathery fronds that are bright green

Habitat:Grows in humid tropical forests.Prefers shaded, moist, and warm conditions with high humidity.

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Gleicheniales, Gleicheniaceae, Dicranopteris

Key Features:
- Fronds are repeatedly forked (dichotomous branching), forming a Y-shaped pattern
- Fronds are stiff, wiry, and spreading, often forming tangled thickets
- Pinnae are narrow, linear, and evenly arranged along each branch
- Rachis is green to brownish and lacks a central midrib after forking
- Young fronds are not tightly coiled like typical fern fiddleheads

Habitat:Common in tropical and subtropical regions.Grows in open, disturbed areas such as roadsides, hillslopes, forest edges, and abandoned land

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Schizaeales, Lygodiaceae, Lygodium

Key Features:
- Unique among ferns: fronds grow indefinitely like a vine, twining around objects
- Leaves are compound, with many small leaflets along a slender rachis
- Leaflets are thin, green, and often slightly lobed

Habitat:Tropical and subtropical forests.

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Polypodiaceae, Pyrrosia

Key Features:
- Fronds are simple, undivided, and leathery in texture
- Fronds are usually lanceolate to elliptic in shape
- Upper leaf surface is green, while the underside is covered with brown to silvery hairs or scales
- Venation is obscure or not clearly visible
- Sori are round and scattered on the underside of mature fronds, without indusia

Habitat:Frequently grows as an epiphyte on tree trunks and branches, but may also occur on rocks.

Taxonomy:Polypodiopsida, Polypodiales, Athyriaceae, Diplazium

Key Features:
- Pinnate fronds with many paired pinnae along the rachis
- Leaflets are elongated with lobed or serrated margins
- Fresh green coloration and soft-textured fronds

Habitat:Thrives in tropical and subtropical regions. Prefers damp, rich soil with high humidity and low direct sunlight
